# Study Guide Ranking — Worst to Best

Every study guide in the repo (including `k8s/`), ranked **worst to best** against one criterion: **is this textbook-quality material with broad scope?** Each guide was scored on two axes:

- **Textbook quality** — does it *explain* with prose, motivation, and mental models (vs. bullet-point syllabi, command cheat-sheets, or code dumps with thin commentary)? Pedagogical progression, worked examples, trade-off discussion, accuracy.
- **Breadth of scope** — does it cover the whole subject the title promises, the way a textbook would, or only a slice?

Scores are out of 100. Ties are ordered by judgment on the two axes.

> **Update (June 2026):** the six guides that originally formed the bottom tier — Terraform, SvelteKit, Next.js, Django, Vue, and Qt — were bullet-point syllabi (little or no code, no prose development). They have since been **rewritten as prose-first textbooks** with embedded code and official + third-party documentation links, and are re-scored below at their new positions. A second pass rewrote **POSTGRES_EXTENSIONS.md** (formerly ranked #1 worst at 55) with per-extension mechanisms and documentation links; it is re-scored at its new position below, as is **ADVANCED_RUST_STUDY_GUIDE.md** (formerly 62), rewritten from note-form bullets into a full prose textbook, and **CLOUDFLARE_STUDY_GUIDE.md** (formerly 71), rewritten from a product map into a mechanism-first treatment. Six guides written in June 2026 — the CB8 iOS/Android pair, Enterprise-Grade APIs, Compiler and Language Internals, Database Internals, and Distributed Algorithms — are now ranked alongside the originals. A further pass rewrote **k8s/KUBERNETES_SECURITY_STUDY_GUIDE.md** (formerly ranked #1 worst at 57) from a bullet checklist into a manifest-driven prose textbook; it is re-scored at its new position below, as is **WEB_LLM_SECURITY_STUDY_GUIDE.md** (formerly 58), rewritten from compressed bullets into a prose-first textbook with each bug class developed in full. **KALI_LINUX_STUDY_GUIDE.md** (formerly 66) was likewise rewritten from a tool catalog into a technique-first kill-chain textbook and re-scored below.
